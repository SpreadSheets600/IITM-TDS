Chinese ink wash painting (Shuimo), Tang-to-Qing dynasty landscape tradition, depicting a long chain of mountain ridges receding into mist from foreground to far distance. In the nearest foreground, a messenger on foot is rendered in dark, confident brushwork calling toward the next ridge; on each successive ridge another tiny human figure relays the message onward, but each figure is lighter, softer, and less distinct than the one before. By the farthest ridge, the final figure is barely visible, almost dissolved into blank paper and pale ink haze, suggesting the signal has nearly vanished. Use monochrome ink with subtle grey washes only, expressive calligraphic brush lines, large areas of deliberate empty paper, atmospheric perspective through ink dilution rather than Western shading, asymmetrical mountain composition, quiet contemplative mood. Visible brush texture on rock faces and mist transitions.
